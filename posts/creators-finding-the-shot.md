If you make videos for a living, or even as a serious hobby, you have folders of raw footage that have grown faster than your memory for what's in them. The cliché of an editor sitting at their desk dragging the playhead back and forth, half-watching themselves, isn't a joke. It's most of the job.

Scrubless puts a Google-like search box on top of that footage. Instead of *"I think the laugh was around minute 18 of clip 7,"* you type *"the part where I'm laughing"* and click the moment that comes back.

## A workflow that doesn't fight the editor

Most editors I know batch-record before they cut. Three days of B-roll at a cabin. A weekend at a conference. Eight one-on-one interviews. Then they sit down on Monday and try to remember what they actually got.

The Scrubless library mode is built for that pile:

1. Open the library card on the landing page and pick the folder.
2. Wait while it indexes (a few minutes per hour of footage on the first pass, set it and grab coffee).
3. Search across the whole folder at once.

You can ask things like:

- *"the wide shot of the lake at sunset"*
- *"when she says she didn't expect that to work"*
- *"the moment the dog comes back inside"*
- *"someone laughs really hard"*

Each result shows the file, the timestamp, a match score, and a thumbnail. Click and you're at the second.

## Where it really pays back

**Pulling clips for social.** You shot 90 minutes of an interview and you want the three quotable moments for TikTok. Type *"the surprising story"*, *"the one-liner about money"*, *"the part where they get emotional"*, mark the moments, hit **Make highlight reel**, and ffmpeg stitches them into one MP4 you can post.

**Searching by what's visible, not just what's said.** This is the one most editors don't expect. If you have skate footage and you want *"the trick where she lands sideways,"* there's no spoken cue to find that, the audio is just wind. Scrubless looks at the frames too, so visual queries land on visual moments.

**Asking questions across a project.** *"Which of our interviewees said anything about their family?"* gets you a written answer with clickable timestamps. Skipping straight to the right moment beats opening Premiere and using its slow find-by-transcript.

## What this isn't

It's not an editor. It's not a transcript service. It's a way to find the moment, after which you do whatever you'd normally do, drop the timestamp into your NLE, screenshot the frame, export the clip.

It's also intentionally not opinionated about your folder structure. Drop in any directory of `.mp4`, `.mov`, `.mkv`, `.webm`, `.m4v`. Scrubless reads what's there.
